# Product

## Register

product

## Users

FamilyFund serves families, small groups, and NGO-style contribution pools where financial responsibility is shared across members. Primary users are super admins who configure the family account, financial secretaries who record and reconcile payments, regular members who check their own contribution status and family-level summaries, and platform super admins who oversee the SaaS operation.

Users often open the app in short operational moments: confirming whether a payment was made, recording a contribution during a conversation, checking overdue members before a reminder, reviewing fund balance, or producing a transparent report for the group.

## Product Purpose

FamilyFund exists to make recurring family or group contributions transparent, accountable, and easy to manage. It tracks members, categories, expected contributions, payments, partial balances, overdue status, reminders, reports, fund adjustments, expenses, subscriptions, and family-level communication.

Success means financial secretaries can record payments quickly and accurately, members can trust what they see, admins can preserve financial history without manual spreadsheets, and the whole group has a clear shared view of contribution progress.

## Brand Personality

Calm, trustworthy, practical.

The product should feel steady and work-focused: a quiet financial operations tool that respects sensitive family money, reduces arguments, and makes accountability feel normal rather than punitive. Copy should be clear, direct, and reassuring, with enough warmth to fit a family context but enough restraint to support real financial records.

## Anti-references

Avoid fintech-corporate gloss, childish family imagery, flashy SaaS gradients, ornamental dashboard cards, generic AI-generated hero patterns, and anything that makes the money workflow feel gamified or unserious. The interface should not look like a bank trying to sell a product, a social app, or a decorative startup landing page.

## Design Principles

1. Trust before delight: financial status, balances, permissions, and histories must feel legible, stable, and auditable.
2. Fast operational paths: recording payments, checking overdue members, inviting members, and reviewing reports should be reachable without ceremony.
3. Privacy by role: show enough aggregate context to build confidence while protecting individual contribution details from users who should not see them.
4. Quiet accountability: overdue and partial states should be unmistakable without shaming users or turning reminders into noise.
5. Mobile-first reliability: the app should remain usable in short, on-the-go sessions with PWA, offline, notification, and touch interactions treated as first-class.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, focus visibility, keyboard access, and readable states. Support reduced motion and avoid relying on color alone for financial status. Keep typography clear on small screens, preserve 16px inputs to avoid mobile zoom, and make sensitive workflows understandable for users who are not technically or financially specialized.
